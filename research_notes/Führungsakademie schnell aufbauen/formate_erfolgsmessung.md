## 1. What does meta-analytic evidence say about leadership training effectiveness, and which design features work?

### Takeaway
Leadership training works, and it works best on behaviour transfer. Lacerenza et al. (2017, JAP, 335 samples) report δ = .63 reactions, .73 learning, .82 transfer, .72 results. Effects are larger when programs have a needs analysis, practice-based methods with feedback, several delivery methods, sessions spaced over time, on-site/face-to-face delivery, and are not self-administered. Transfer research (Blume et al. 2010) adds that the work environment (manager and peer support) and trainee motivation strongly predict transfer, and these predictors matter more for "open" skills such as leadership.

### Cited Findings
- **Lacerenza, Reyes, Marlow, Joseph & Salas (2017), "Leadership Training Design, Delivery, and Implementation: A Meta-Analysis", Journal of Applied Psychology 102(12), 1686–1718, doi 10.1037/apl0000241.** Uses Kirkpatrick's four criteria and tests 15 design/delivery/implementation moderators. — [UCF STARS record](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/scopus2015/5360); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Leadership-Training-Design,-Delivery,-and-A-Lacerenza-Reyes/6d1f3a28d8a5f569bd09eee367b40f6e2c731fd2); [author PDF hosted by Rice/Doerr Institute](https://doerr.rice.edu/sites/g/files/bxs5941/files/2026-01/Leadership%20Training%20Design,%20Delivery,%20and%20Implementation.pdf)
- Overall effects: δ = .63 (reactions), .73 (learning), .82 (transfer), .72 (results), from 335 independent samples. Transfer had the largest effect. — [Quarterdeck summary](https://quarterdeck.co.uk/articles/leadership-training-design-delivery-and-implementation-a-meta-analysis/); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Leadership-Training-Design,-Delivery,-and-A-Lacerenza-Reyes/6d1f3a28d8a5f569bd09eee367b40f6e2c731fd2). One secondary site instead gives "d = .28–.63" for the same paper. That figure looks like a misquote; the δ values above match the published abstract (verify).
- Moderators that increase effectiveness, per the published abstract: needs analysis, feedback, multiple delivery methods (especially practice), spaced training sessions, on-site location, and face-to-face delivery that is not self-administered. — [Semantic Scholar / ResearchGate abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318737359_Leadership_Training_Design_Delivery_and_Implementation_A_Meta-Analysis)
- The 2015 dissertation version ("The Nuts and Bolts of Leadership Training") reported different details. For **transfer**, the largest effects came from needs-analysis-based content, information-, demonstration- and practice-based methods, feedback, face-to-face settings and **voluntary** attendance. For **results**, the largest effects came from longer programs that were **mandatory**, held in weekly (spaced) sessions, practice-based and on-site. — [UCF ETD 5162](https://stars.library.ucf.edu/etd/5162/). Caveat: the voluntary-versus-mandatory result is not stated in the published abstract, so it is unconfirmed for the 2017 version (verify the tables).
- A UK government rapid evidence review on management training in government found trainer-facilitated delivery beats self-administered training, and calls this one of the more robust findings. Face-to-face beats virtual, but that evidence is more mixed. Longer and practice-focused programs tend to do better. For most design features, the evidence in prior studies is mixed. — [GOV.UK rapid evidence review](https://gov.uk/government/publications/effective-management-training-in-government/what-do-we-know-about-the-design-of-effective-management-training-in-government-a-rapid-evidence-review-to-inform-training-design-and-evaluation)
- **Blume, Ford, Baldwin & Huang (2010), "Transfer of training: A meta-analytic review", Journal of Management 36(4)** (one source lists vol. 39; verify). 89 studies. Transfer was positively related to cognitive ability, conscientiousness, motivation and a supportive work environment. Most predictors were **stronger for open skills (e.g. leadership)** than for closed skills (e.g. software). Same-source, same-context measurement inflated relationships, so self-reported transfer overstates effects. — [Wikipedia: Transfer of training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_training); [Scinapse](https://www.scinapse.io/papers/2165041900); [Baldwin/Ford/Blume follow-up, U Michigan Deep Blue](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/136388/hrdq21278_am.pdf?sequence=2)
- **Coaching (Jones, Woods & Guillaume 2016, JOOP 89(2), 249–277).** Positive effects of workplace coaching on learning and performance outcomes. Effects were larger with **internal** coaches than external coaches. Using multi-source (360°) feedback within coaching was linked to *smaller* effects. Format (face-to-face vs. blended) and duration did not moderate effects. — [Univ. of Reading CentAUR](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/74522/)
- **Coaching (Theeboom, Beersma & van Vianen 2014).** Later reviews describe coaching's effects on individual outcomes as medium to large. Within-subject designs gave larger effects than independent-group designs. — [VU research portal](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/does-coaching-work-a-meta-analysis-on-the-effects-of-coaching-on-/); [Frontiers in Psychology 2023 meta-review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1089797/pdf)
- RCT-only coaching meta-analysis (Burt & Talati 2017): moderate effect, g = 0.42, lower than earlier reviews, attributed to methodology. — [Emerald meta-review](https://www.emerald.com/view-large/78907393); [Frontiers 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1089797/pdf)
- **70-20-10 critique.** The ratio is usually traced to CCL research summarised in McCall, Lombardo & Morrison, *Lessons of Experience* (1988), which never uses the phrase. Lombardo & Eichinger restated it in the *Career Architect Development Planner* (1996). The basis is retrospective self-reports of 191 successful executives. Kajewski & Madsen (2012) found a lack of empirical data and an uncertain origin. Clardy (2018, HRDR) calls it "a fact in search of evidence". ATD's view: useful as a loose guideline ("people learn constantly on the job"), not a scientific ratio. Rigid application risks treating formal and informal learning as separate. — [ATD: 70-20-10 Where Is the Evidence?](https://www.td.org/insights/70-20-10-where-is-the-evidence); [Wikipedia 70/20/10](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/70/20/10_Model); [HR Grapevine: 4 shortcomings](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/content/article/insight-2017-06-28-the-4-shortcomings-of-70-20-10)
- **McKinsey, "Why leadership-development programs fail" (Gurdjian, Halbeisen & Lane, Jan 2014).** Four common mistakes: overlooking context, decoupling reflection from real work, underestimating mind-sets, and failing to measure results. Remedies: focus on a small number of capabilities critical to the business, tie development to real on-the-job projects with business impact, address mind-sets, and track results. Example: an engineering and construction firm developed 300 mid-level leaders while keeping projects on time and on budget. — [McKinsey Quarterly](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/leadership/why-leadership-development-programs-fail)
- **AI practice and roleplay.** The only academic evidence found is qualitative. Managers valued low-risk adaptive simulations for difficult conversations but raised concerns about the consistency of feedback, realism and bias, and open-endedness (Wilhelm et al., arXiv May 2025, CommCoach). — [arXiv 2505.14452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14452v2). An Attensi survey (n = 2,139) found 75% of employees reported anxiety from difficult conversations and 72% would welcome more practice. This shows demand, not effectiveness. — [Training Industry](https://trainingindustry.com/articles/learning-technologies/ai-driven-role-play-training-and-the-agentic-ai-bringing-virtual-humans-to-the-workplace/)

### Inferences
- The design checklist the evidence supports: (1) needs analysis tied to strategy, (2) practice with feedback (role-plays, simulations, AI practice) rather than lectures, (3) spaced cohort modules over weeks or months, (4) facilitated rather than pure self-paced e-learning, (5) on-site or work-embedded application (real projects, action learning), (6) building the work environment (involve the participants' managers, peer support) because transfer for leadership is driven by context.
- Coaching evidence favours internal coaches. That supports building a pool of internal coaches or peer coaches as a cheap, effective format.
- AI roleplay fits the strongest lever (practice plus feedback) cheaply, but evidence of transfer for it is still missing. Pilot it with an in-house pre/post or control-group measurement rather than assuming impact.
- Present 70-20-10 to stakeholders as a design heuristic ("most development happens at work, so design the work part"), never as evidence.

### Gaps
- Could not read Lacerenza (2017) moderator tables. Exact effect sizes per moderator, and the published direction for voluntary vs. mandatory and virtual vs. face-to-face, need verification against the PDF (author copy linked above via Rice/Doerr).
- No exact pooled effect sizes found for Theeboom (2014) or Jones (2016). Smither et al. (2005) 360° feedback meta-analysis not reached by search.
- No meta-analytic evidence found specifically on microlearning, peer coaching or action learning for leaders in this session.
- No controlled outcome studies found for AI roleplay in manager training (as of searches in Nov 2026).

## 2. What curriculum architectures do academies use (by leadership level; leading self/others/organization; Leadership Pipeline)?

### Takeaway
The most common architecture is level-based and follows Charan, Drotter & Noel's *Leadership Pipeline*: from leading self to leading others (first-line managers), then managers of managers, functional, business, group and enterprise leaders. Each transition requires new skills, time allocation and work values. The CCL-style framing "leading self, leading others, leading the organization" condenses the same idea into three tiers. Key current skill gaps (DDI 2025) are strategy and change management, while AI readiness is a new curriculum demand (HBI 2025).

### Cited Findings
- Leadership Pipeline: the first passage (individual contributor to manager of others) is often called the hardest. The shift is from doing the work to getting work done through others. Skills for this passage: planning work, filling jobs, assigning work, motivating, coaching and measuring others' work. In the second passage (managing managers), the leader stops doing technical work, selects and develops first-line managers and holds them accountable for managerial work. — [Shortform summary](https://shortform.com/blog/?p=125361); [O'Reilly book ch. 2](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-leadership-pipeline/9780470894569/ch02.html); [RISUS journal article](https://revistas.pucsp.br/risus/article/view/56955)
- The model is usually described as six passages, but some sources describe seven transitions or rename levels. Verify against the 2nd edition (Jossey-Bass, 2011) before using level names. — [Glasshouse PDF summary](https://cms.theglasshouse.org/ProductPdf/mL1C75/600892/Leadership%20Pipeline%20Ram%20Charan.pdf); [Voitto summary](https://blog.voitto.com.br/resumo/en/the-leadership-pipeline)
- **DDI Global Leadership Forecast 2025** (11th edition; 2,185 HR professionals and 10,796 leaders; 50+ countries, 24 industries). Leaders name **setting strategy** and **managing change** as their biggest skill gaps, but only 22% of HR teams prioritise these skills. Trust in immediate managers fell to 29%. 40% of stressed leaders have considered leaving leadership. Frontline managers are 3× more likely than senior leaders to worry about AI's impact. High-potential individual contributors' intent to leave rose from 13% (2020) to 21% (2024), and high-potentials without regular development are about 3.7–4× more likely to leave (sources vary). More than half of CEOs rank talent recruitment and retention as their top concern. — [HRO Today](https://www.hrotoday.com/news/ticker/nearly-half-of-leaders-consider-leaving-to-improve-well-being/); [SHRM vendor directory press release](https://vendordirectory.shrm.org/company/779954/news/3568735/new-ddi-study-signals-looming-leadership-exodus-with-71-reporting-increased-stress); [Outsource Accelerator](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/global-leadership-crisis-stress/); German-language report: [DDI GLF 2025 (DE) PDF](https://media.ddiworld.com/research/glf-2025-report-german.pdf)
- **Harvard Business Impact, 2025 Global Leadership Development Study** (1,100+ L&D professionals, 14 countries; subtitle "Fast, Fluid, and Future-Focused"). 56% of organisations expect leaders to use AI in strategic decision-making, yet many lack training to do so. A 2026 edition exists. — [HBI 2025 findings](https://www.harvardbusiness.org/insight/2025-global-leadership-development-study-research-findings/); [PDF](https://www.harvardbusiness.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/HBI_2025-Global-Leadership-Development-Study_Research-Findings_May25.pdf)
- German practitioner guidance (Deutscher Personalwirtschaftspreis 2024 learnings): do not reduce leadership development to knowledge transfer; use holistic, experience-based formats; use exchange and collegial (peer) consultation between leaders in flexible, work-near formats. — [Personalwirtschaft](https://www.personalwirtschaft.de/?p=187209); [Personalwirtschaft](https://www.personalwirtschaft.de/?p=187926)
- Haufe Akademie describes a six-step process from needs analysis to continuous evaluation ("Return on Learning"). — [Haufe Akademie blog](https://www.haufe-akademie.de/blog/themen/fuehrung-und-leadership/fuehrungskraefteentwicklung/)

### Inferences
- A practical academy architecture: **Track 1, first-line / new leaders** (leading others: role transition, feedback, delegation, 1:1s, difficult conversations, basic labour law); **Track 2, mid-level / leaders of leaders** (developing leaders, cross-functional collaboration, change management, strategy execution); **Track 3, senior / high-potential** (strategy, transformation, AI-enabled decision-making, enterprise thinking), plus a **"leading self" foundation** for all, and, for sales and functional academies, role-specific skill tracks on the same chassis.
- DDI's gap data suggests putting "managing change" and "setting strategy" into mid and senior tracks early. The high-potential flight-risk data supports a dedicated high-potential track as a retention lever.
- Making the "70" part (assignments, projects) and "20" part (peer consultation, coaching) explicit in each track turns 70-20-10 from a slogan into design.

### Gaps
- No primary CCL source on "leading self/others/organization" reached. The framing is widely used, but not verified here.
- No published DACH academy curriculum with outcome data found.

## 3. How can formats be built quickly and cheaply (cohort-based, internal faculty, virtual live, AI practice)?

### Takeaway
The cheapest high-evidence formats are facilitated cohorts with spaced sessions, practice and feedback, internal coaches and peer-consultation groups, and work-embedded projects. Virtual live is acceptable but evidence on it is mixed versus face-to-face. Pure self-paced e-learning is the weakest option. AI roleplay is a low-cost way to scale practice, but evidence on it is still qualitative.

### Cited Findings
- Facilitated beats self-administered delivery; face-to-face beats virtual, though the virtual evidence is mixed. — [GOV.UK review](https://gov.uk/government/publications/effective-management-training-in-government/what-do-we-know-about-the-design-of-effective-management-training-in-government-a-rapid-evidence-review-to-inform-training-design-and-evaluation); [Lacerenza 2017 abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318737359_Leadership_Training_Design_Delivery_and_Implementation_A_Meta-Analysis)
- Internal coaches produced larger effects than external coaches. Format (face-to-face vs. blended) did not moderate coaching effects. — [Jones et al. 2016](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/74522/)
- Google (Project Oxygen) turned internal people-analytics findings into new-manager training, one-on-one coaching by **in-house** coaches, and twice-yearly upward-feedback surveys. Google reported a statistically significant gain in manager quality for 75% of its worst-performing managers. There is no published control group or sample. — [NYT 2011 article (PDF copy)](https://community.mis.temple.edu/ruimis/files/2013/06/Google%C3%A2%E2%82%AC%E2%84%A2s-Quest-to-Build-a-Better-Boss.pdf); [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/michael-schneider/analysis-10000-reports-told-google-to-train-new-managers-6-areas.html)
- A US federal (GSA OES) randomised test of growth-mindset tools in a manager program partly modelled on Project Oxygen found **no detectable effect**, with imprecise estimates due to low response rates. — [GSA Office of Evaluation Sciences](https://oes.gsa.gov/projects/project-management)
- AI roleplay vendors claim, for example, 20 practice conversations in the time of one workshop. These are marketing claims without outcome data. — [Digital Humans blog](https://www.digitalhumans.com/blog/how-to-train-managers-for-difficult-conversations); adaptive difficulty described in [HR Brew 2025](https://hr-brew.com/stories/2025/05/06/ai-coaches-managers-development)
- Mittelstand example format: 5 single training days spread over about six months for team leaders at a streetwear importer (design only, no results). — [AV Fallstudie PDF](https://assets-global.website-files.com/64c449d2534786a275f66a0e/652ea0ac374d39685eedbce7_AV_Fallstudie_2310.pdf)

### Inferences
- A low-cost blueprint that matches the evidence: a cohort of 12–20 participants; 4–6 half-day or full-day modules spaced every 3–6 weeks (virtual live or on-site); between modules, peer-consultation triads ("kollegiale Beratung"), a real transfer project sponsored by the participant's manager, and AI roleplay practice. Internal senior leaders act as faculty and sponsors; internal coaches are trained. Start with the first-line track because the pipeline's first transition is the hardest and the cohort is the largest.
- The Google-vs-GSA contrast shows that copying a famous program does not guarantee results. Build measurement in from the start.

### Gaps
- No cost benchmarks (EUR per participant) for internal vs. external academy formats found.
- No rigorous evidence found on microlearning for leadership behaviour change.

## 4. Which KPIs and dashboards are recommended; what are realistic ROI figures; how to present value to the board?

### Takeaway
Use a chain of evidence rather than a single ROI number: Kirkpatrick/Phillips levels or LTEM tiers for learning and transfer, plus business KPIs (manager-level engagement, retention of leaders and their teams, internal promotion and succession coverage, and unit performance). Phillips ROI is credible when the program is isolated and costs are fully loaded, but published figures vary widely, and the best documented range, 147%–633%, comes from general training rather than leadership specifically. Brinkerhoff's Success Case Method gives fast, story-based evidence that boards find persuasive.

### Cited Findings
- **LTEM (Thalheimer).** Eight tiers: 1 attendance, 2 activity, 3 learner perceptions, 4 knowledge, 5 decision-making competence, 6 task competence, 7 transfer, 8 effects of transfer. Only tiers 5–8 validate that learning occurred. Most course evaluations stop at tier 4 or lower. LTEM was built in response to the Kirkpatrick-Katzell model's gaps and emphasises a causal pathway from learning to job performance to results. — [Learning Guild](https://www.learningguild.com/?p=6987); [Thiagi: Seven applications of LTEM](https://www.thiagi.com/articles/2019/9/25/seven-applications-for-thalheimers-ltem-learning-transfer-evaluation-model); [Hone interview with Thalheimer](https://honehq.com/resources/blog/debunking-learning-myths-will-thalheimer)
- **Phillips ROI method.** Convert business-impact changes to money, subtract fully loaded direct and indirect costs, and divide net benefits by costs. Only 18% of organisations gather relevant business-impact metrics. — [SHRM, "How to Measure the ROI of Leadership Development"](https://shrm.org/topics-tools/news/hr-magazine/how-to-measure-roi-leadership-development)
- Across 22 companies that calculated training ROI, results ranged from **147% to 633%**. These were general training programs, not leadership specifically. — [Chief Learning Officer, "Stepping Up to the ROI Challenge" (2018)](https://www.chieflearningofficer.com/2018/10/05/stepping-up-to-the-roi-challenge/)
- ROI Institute books with leadership case studies: *Measuring Leadership Development* (McGraw-Hill 2012; coaching and cross-functional/production manager cases) and *Measuring the Success of Leadership Development* (ATD 2015; e.g. an IAMGOLD supervisory leadership program). — [Skillsoft listing](https://www.skillsoft.com/book/measuring-leadership-development-quantify-your-programs-impact-and-roi-on-organizational-performance-0a070b10-f3b6-11e6-bb2f-0242c0a80b05); [Skillsoft listing](https://www.skillsoft.com/book/measuring-the-success-of-leadership-development-a-step-by-step-guide-for-measuring-impact-and-calculating-roi-c6d147f0-f37b-11e6-bb29-0242c0a80506)
- An academic case calculated the monetary ROI of a leadership development program. — [Journal of Leadership Education](https://journalofleadershiped.org/jole_articles/return-on-investment-roi-calculating-the-monetary-return-of-a-leadership-development-program/)
- Widely circulated figures such as "IBM 707% ROI" or "IBM 300%" have no traceable primary source. Do not use them. — [Humansmart blog](https://blogs.humansmart.com.mx/blog-how-can-companies-measure-the-true-roi-of-leadership-development-programs-56161)
- **Brinkerhoff Success Case Method** (*The Success Case Method*, 2003; *Telling Training's Story*, 2006). Steps: (1) focus and plan the study and build an impact model defining what success looks like; (2) survey all participants briefly to identify the most and least successful users; (3) interview success and non-success cases; (4) document verified impact and barriers; (5) communicate stories and recommendations. Sources vary on the exact step count. Strength: fast, shows what works and which organisational factors (e.g. manager support) block transfer. — [Berrett-Koehler excerpt](https://www.bkconnection.com/static/The_Success_Case_Method_EXCERPT.pdf); [Watershed](https://www.watershedlrs.com/blog/learning-evaluation/brinkerhoff-method/); [TrainingZone: SCM for sales training](https://trainingzone.co.uk/evaluating-sales-training-with-brinkerhoffs-success-case-method/)
- **KPIs (vendor guidance, indicative).** Leading indicators: internal promotion rate, development-plan participation, high-potential identification accuracy, succession coverage ratio. Lagging: retention of developed leaders over 12–24 months after the program, and manager-level engagement tracked quarterly. — [exec.com, Leadership development KPIs](https://www.exec.com/learn/leadership-development-kpis)
- Gallup data cited for the German Mittelstand: 18%–43% lower turnover with high emotional commitment (engagement). This is not a direct effect of leadership development. — [Markt und Mittelstand](https://www.marktundmittelstand.de/ratgeber/fuehrungskrise-im-mittelstand)
- High-potentials without regular development are about 3.7–4× more likely to leave (DDI GLF 2025). This can monetise the retention case. — [Outsource Accelerator](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/global-leadership-crisis-stress/); [HRO Today](https://www.hrotoday.com/news/ticker/nearly-half-of-leaders-consider-leaving-to-improve-well-being/)
- Transfer self-reports inflate effects (same-source bias). Use manager or direct-report ratings, or objective data. — [Wikipedia: Transfer of training, citing Blume et al. 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_of_training)

### Inferences
- **Recommended dashboard (three layers):**
  - *Delivery and learning (LTEM 1–6):* participation and completion; learner perception focused on usefulness rather than satisfaction; scenario-based decision tests; observed task competence in roleplay or AI simulation.
  - *Transfer (LTEM 7):* 90-day behaviour change rated by direct reports and the participant's own manager (pulse 360°), plus transfer-project completion.
  - *Business impact (LTEM 8 / Kirkpatrick 4 / Phillips 4–5):* team engagement score of participants vs. non-participants; regretted attrition in participants' teams; retention of participants; internal fill rate for leadership roles; succession coverage; time-to-productivity of new leaders; for sales academies, quota attainment, win rate and ramp time.
- **For the board:** lead with 2–3 business KPIs and a comparison group (cohort vs. not yet trained, staggered roll-out), add 2–3 success-case stories (Brinkerhoff), and calculate a conservative Phillips ROI on one hard metric (e.g. avoided turnover cost of leaders and their teams), with an isolation factor and confidence adjustment. Avoid uncited ROI claims (e.g. IBM figures).
- Staggered cohort roll-out creates a natural waiting-list control group at no extra cost.

### Gaps
- No verified leadership-specific Phillips ROI percentage from a primary case found in this session (book case studies not accessible).
- No Bersin/Deloitte, Lighthouse or LinkedIn 2024–2025 primary benchmark data on internal promotion rates reached. A LinkedIn 2024 figure ("internal movers 64% more likely to stay 3 years") was seen only second-hand.
- No Personalwirtschaft or managerSeminare article with a quantified evaluation found.

## 5. Concrete case studies with measured results (ideally DACH and mid-sized)

### Takeaway
Publicly documented cases with rigorous measured outcomes are rare, and no DACH or Mittelstand case with published quantitative results was found. The best-known measured case (Google Project Oxygen) relies on internal, uncontrolled data. A controlled public-sector replication of a related intervention found no effect. Case evidence should therefore be generated internally rather than borrowed.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Project Oxygen:** 8 (later 10) manager behaviours derived from people analytics; training, in-house coaching and upward feedback. 75% of the worst-performing managers showed a statistically significant improvement in manager quality (internal reporting, no control group). — [NYT 2011 PDF copy](https://community.mis.temple.edu/ruimis/files/2013/06/Google%C3%A2%E2%82%AC%E2%84%A2s-Quest-to-Build-a-Better-Boss.pdf)
- **US GSA / OES:** randomised evaluation of growth-mindset tools for federal managers; no detectable effect. — [OES](https://oes.gsa.gov/projects/project-management)
- **McKinsey example:** an engineering and construction firm's multiyear program developed 300 mid-level leaders while delivering projects on time and on budget (no further metrics). — [McKinsey 2014](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/leadership/why-leadership-development-programs-fail)
- **DACH Mittelstand (process only, no outcome data):** a southern German machine builder (about 800 employees) realigned its leadership culture after a change of ownership. — [Manager Institut case study](https://www.manager-institut.de/fallstudie-fuehrungskraefteentwicklung-in-transformationsphasen/). A team-leader program at a streetwear importer used 5 days over about six months. — [AV Fallstudie PDF](https://assets-global.website-files.com/64c449d2534786a275f66a0e/652ea0ac374d39685eedbce7_AV_Fallstudie_2310.pdf). That PDF also cites a "256%" effect attributed to a Harvard/Michigan study, which could not be verified. Do not use it.
- **Deutscher Personalwirtschaftspreis 2024:** 115 HR projects submitted; a best-practice collection was published from the longlist and is a possible source for DACH cases. — [Personalwirtschaft](https://www.personalwirtschaft.de/?p=187209)
- **Mittelstand context:** Markt und Mittelstand reports a shortage of leaders in the Mittelstand, and Gallup data show German leadership quality at a historic low. — [Markt und Mittelstand: Dem Mittelstand gehen die Führungskräfte aus](https://www.marktundmittelstand.de/personal/dem-mittelstand-gehen-die-fuehrungskraefte-aus); [Markt und Mittelstand: Gallup-Studie](https://www.marktundmittelstand.de/ratgeber/fuehrungskrise-im-mittelstand)

### Inferences
- For a fast-built academy, the credible path is to generate your own evidence: baseline KPIs before the start, a staggered cohort design, a 90-day transfer pulse, and Success Case interviews after 6 months.
- ROI Institute case books, the Personalwirtschaftspreis best-practice collection and the Doerr Institute (Rice University, which publishes leadership-development evaluation research) are the most promising sources for verified case numbers.

### Gaps
- No DACH or mid-sized company case with published quantitative outcomes (turnover, engagement, promotion rates) found. Searches in German (Personalwirtschaft, managerSeminare, Mittelstand) returned process descriptions only.
- Sales-academy case studies with measured results (e.g. win rate or ramp time) were not searched in depth due to the tool-call budget.
- Full DDI GLF 2025 and HBI 2025 reports could not be fetched (host blocked), so detailed numbers on the effectiveness of development methods (e.g. coaching, simulations, assessments) from these reports are missing.
